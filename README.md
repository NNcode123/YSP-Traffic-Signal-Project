# YSP-Traffic-Signal-Project

Artificial intelligence (AI) systems have become increasingly prevalent in society, which can especially be seen in the explosive growth of numerous industries, including autonomous vehicles.  Autonomous vehicles utilize neural-network, visual based recognition systems to correctly identify objects and respond accordingly. Therefore, neural networks enable autonomous vehicles to correctly identify and react to pedestrians, road signs, and traffic lights. While neural networks in autonomous vehicles have improved transportation, they form the basis of Visual AI processes and could therefore be fooled by the introduction of extraneous elements on roadways.   These attacks can have catastrophic impacts on the occupants in the vehicle and are oftentimes fatal. For example, neural networks could be manipulated into overestimating the speed limit posted by a sign, potentially leading to catastrophic crashes.  
This research will focus on using adversarial attacks to compromise the integrity of autonomous vehicles, particularly by interfering with conventional road signs through the introduction of slight color variations from the original road sign. There are many fundamental types of cybersecurity attacks, but investigating adversarial attacks because they are the most threatening type of attack; specifically, they introduce imperceptible changes in images to the human eye and are often very difficult to detect.
The main objective of this research is to investigate the efficacy of undetected, targeted adversarial attacks on fooling neural networks designed to identify traffic signs. However, I aim for the findings of this research to be instrumental in helping refine the existing defense system’s ability to counteract adversarial attacks. An increasing number of the U.S. population has become dependent on using autonomous vehicles for everyday transport. Therefore, it is important to research these types of attacks to significantly lower the chance of catastrophic events in the future. 
Proposed Question- To what extent can a targeted adversarial attack on the GTSRB data set successfully fool a neural network without detection from the user?
To answer the question, I will employ and train a neural network with sample road signs. Then, an adversarial attack will be performed on the neural network to determine the proportion of incorrect identifications produced. If the target neural network has a large incorrect identification rate of the road signs, I can be convinced that my question was answered. However, If the neural network has a lower incorrect identification rate of road signs than expected, then I would be less convinced of the efficacy of my attack.
